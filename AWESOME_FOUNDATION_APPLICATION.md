### 500-Word Description (MUST stay under 500 words)

```
My son turned 10 on a day I couldn't be with him. Supervised visitation — two
hours a week, no exceptions. So I built him a game instead.

BONDING is an open-source chemistry game designed specifically for neurodivergent
learners. It uses tetrahedral geometry — the same minimum enclosing structure
found in atoms, crystals, and brain tissue — as its core gameplay mechanic. Kids
learn chemistry by bonding atoms. The rules are baked into the geometry.

I built it because:

1. Neurodivergent kids get left out of STEM. Traditional chemistry education
   requires reading social cues, following multi-step instructions, and
   tolerating sensory overload. BONDING removes all three barriers.

2. I'm raising two neurodivergent sons while navigating a permanent endocrine
   condition (hypoparathyroidism) and late-diagnosed AuDHD. I know what the
   barriers feel like from the inside.

3. $1,000 doesn't build a game company. It builds one more node in the open-
   source assistive technology mesh.

BONDING is free, offline-capable, and works on any device. It has 426 passing
tests, zero external dependencies, and a deployment pipeline hardened to
enterprise standards. It's not a prototype — it's production software.

The $1,000 would cover:
- Server costs for the next 12 months ($50/month)
- Printing accessible game materials for in-person workshops ($200)
- Accessibility audit by neurodivergent users ($300)
- Ko-fi shop launch (digital downloads to sustain ongoing development) ($450)

P31 Labs, Inc. is a Georgia nonprofit (EIN 42-1888158) with 501(c)(3)
pending. Nothing about us without us. Every design decision comes from lived
neurological experience.

As my son said when he first played: "Dad, this is actually fun." That's the
metric that matters.
```
